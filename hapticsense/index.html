HapticSense

Abstract

A wrist-worn device that stimulates the nerves in your wrist so you can feel virtual objects in VR and AR, without gloves or mechanical actuators.

Year
2025
Length
2 months
Methods
Speculative, industrial and UI design

Read the full process Try the setup prototype

Question

VR looks real, but touching things in it doesn't feel like anything. Haptic gloves are bulky and still miss the feel of an object. Could touch come from the wrist instead?

Research such as the xTouch platform shows that surface electrodes on the wrist can stimulate the median nerve well enough for people to tell hard from soft and large from small objects. HapticSense asks what a consumer device built on that would be like to use.

Process

I started with sketches of where the electrodes, sensors and battery could sit around the wrist, then mapped a user journey from putting the band on to the first touch in VR.

Nerve stimulation feels different for every body, so calibration became the core of the interface. The setup flow has people flex each finger, confirm they feel a test pulse, and tune intensity and immersion.

Outcome

A hardware concept shown in renders and a clickable eight-step setup prototype. Alongside it, a look at how Nintendo's "Touching is good" campaign taught people a new kind of touch, as a model for introducing an unfamiliar sensation.
