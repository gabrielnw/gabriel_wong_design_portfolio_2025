HapticSense

Abstract

A speculative wristband that stimulates the nerves in your wrist so you can feel virtual objects in VR and AR, without gloves. I designed the device and its calibration interface, and built a clickable prototype of the setup flow.

Year
2024
Length
2 months
Role
Solo
Tools
Fusion 360, Blender, paper sketches

Read the full process Try the setup prototype

Question

VR looks convincing, but touching things in it feels like nothing. Haptic gloves are bulky and still miss what an object feels like. Could the sense of touch come from the wrist instead of the fingertips?

The project started in The Interface, an art history course, where I read David Parisi on how touch is culturally learned and J.C.R. Licklider on human–computer symbiosis. If people learned to swipe and pinch, they can learn to read a new kind of touch. The question became what a device that teaches it would look like.

Research

The xTouch study showed that surface electrodes on the wrist can stimulate the median nerve well enough for people to tell hard from soft and large from small objects, without surgery. That set the constraints: electrodes over the median and ulnar nerves, low latency, and a band small and light enough to wear for a session.

Interface

Nerve stimulation feels different on every body, so the hardest design problem is not the band but the first five minutes. I designed the setup as a calibration conversation: the band asks you to flex each finger, sends a test pulse and asks if you felt it, and recalibrates if you didn't. Then you set intensity with a physical dial and immersion with a slider.

Outcome

A hardware concept built in Fusion 360 and Blender, and a clickable prototype of the full eight-step setup. The concept hasn't been tested with people yet.
