The Future of Data Storage

Overview

What if the world's data moved from data centres into DNA? A strategic foresight project that maps four possible futures and ends in an issue of Wired from 2040.

Year
2024
Length
8 months
Role
Solo
Context
Bachelor's thesis, Industrial Design
Methods
Strategic foresight, speculative design
Tools
InDesign, Midjourney, AI-assisted editing

Read the full process Read the magazine (PDF, 31 MB)

Question

The world makes more data every year, and the data centres that hold it use huge amounts of energy while their media wear out within decades. DNA is far denser, can last for centuries, and needs no power while stored. If it became the main way we store data, how would the world change?

Process

I tracked research trends and interviewed a DNA storage researcher at CacheDNA to understand where the technology is likely heading. That set the probable future; the cone of plausibility opened it up from there.

Using Dator's Four Futures, I wrote Growth, Collapse, Discipline and Transformative scenarios, then built out the Transformative one into a world in 2040 where farmland becomes data vaults. I tested it through three sacrificial artifacts: a knowledge capsule, a plant that wilts when your data is unhealthy, and a magazine from the future. I presented all three to a board of futures practitioners. The magazine got the strongest reaction, because it made the future feel believable, so I took it forward.

Outcome

The final piece is the July/August 2040 issue of Wired: 48 pages, designed and printed as a physical magazine. A magazine can hold a whole world at once, so instead of one product the scenario shows up everywhere you turn the page.

The cover story follows Gavin Moore, a Kansas farmer who stores data in genetically altered sunflowers. A feature on rural Minnesota shows former farms turned into bio-synthesis centres. A darker story follows a woman trafficked as a DNA courier, her body carrying a stolen payload. Around them, the everyday texture: an advice column for a reader whose bio-hacked houseplants argue over what to watch, a piece on the hobbyist "user plant interface" scene, and ads for algae fuel and neural subscriptions.

Developed through the Experimental Realism mentorship program with futures practitioners.
